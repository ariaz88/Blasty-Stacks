The core insight

Survival in this game is HP × (100+DEF)/100 — defense multiplies HP, it does not add to it. The current formula adds two quantities that multiply. That single modelling error is the source of almost every problem below.

02

What is wrong today

Every claim here was measured by simulating real matchups using the project's own combat model — 2 hero profiles × 6 levels × 6 enemy archetypes × 6 stages.

⚠ The bias is one-directional, which is worse than noise

Of 40 mistakes, all 40 were "CP said the player wins, the enemy won". Zero went the other way. A player trusting CP will consistently walk into stages believing they are stronger than they are.

A retracted finding

An earlier version of this analysis claimed CP ranks the hero roster backwards. That was wrong and is retracted. It assumed trueDPS = attack × attackSpeed, taken from a field comment without verifying the game implements it — it does not. Real output is attack ÷ 0.6, which makes the ATK-72 profile 12.5% stronger, and CP rates it 9.9% higher. CP ranks those two correctly. The real defect is the 9.8% duel error above.

03

The proposed formula

player wins  ⟺  playerATK × playerAtkSpd × playerEHP  >  enemyATK × enemyAtkSpd × enemyEHP

                 └────────────────── this is CP ──────────────────┘

This is not a heuristic that happens to score well. It is the duel condition rearranged. Both sides kill in targetEHP ÷ myATK hits at a cadence of recoveryTime ÷ myAtkSpd, so comparing times to kill reduces algebraically to comparing ATK × AtkSpd × EHP. Whoever's product is larger wins, by construction.

The helper already exists in your project

CPCalculator.EffectiveHP (CPCalculator.cs:50-54) computes HP × (100+DEF)/100 exactly, mirrors your real damage equation, and has zero call sites. It was written and never used.

Robustness — why not just retune the old weights?

A weighted sum can be tuned to 100% on today's roster (wA 1.00, wH 0.35, wD 0.90). But it is fitted, not structural — it degrades as soon as a unit falls outside current stat ranges, because it treats defense as linear when survivability is multiplicative.

04

Your requirement: does 1 more CP always win?

Short answer

The formula guarantees it. The combat model does not. In continuous maths the guarantee is exact — 0 errors in 200,000 random duels, and provable algebraically. What breaks it is that hits are whole numbers.

Why whole hits break a 1-CP edge

A: ATK 100  HP 300  DEF 50  →  CP 45,000
B: ATK  99  HP 300  DEF 50  →  CP 44,550     (A leads by 450)

A needs ceil(450 / 100) = 5 hits  →  3.000 s
B needs ceil(450 /  99) = 5 hits  →  3.000 s

result: DRAW — both die on the same swing

A's advantage is real but invisible: it did not change the hit count. Until the edge is large enough to remove a whole hit from the kill, it changes nothing.

⚠ The honest answer

"1 more CP always wins" is not achievable with whole-number hits, in any formula — not just this one. At a 0.1% margin the higher-CP unit wins 52.9% of the time. That residue is quantisation, not a flaw in the metric.

But it is fixable — and the lever is fight length

The more hits a kill takes, the finer the granularity and the tighter the guarantee. This is the single most actionable finding in the report.

Raising HP relative to ATK lengthens fights and tightens the guarantee. No formula change needed.

06

Supporting changes required

The formula alone is not enough — two stats it depends on are currently disconnected from the game.

⚠ Two balance landmines

Movement. The sheets say moveSpeed 3.5 (players) and 2.9–3.5 (enemies). The game actually uses 0.5 and 0.2 from Inspector fields. Wiring the stat through naively makes players 7× and enemies 15× faster.

Attack speed. Making cadence recoveryTime / attackSpeed with today's values (2.0 / 1.5) makes heroes 1.5–2× stronger and enemies weaker — every stage becomes dramatically easier.

Both are solved by the same move

Re-author attackSpeed into 0.9–1.3 and set movement SO values to the tuned figures. Cadence then lands at 0.46–0.67 s, straddling today's flat 0.6 s — attack speed becomes a real design lever with no free power jump.

07

The open problem: upgrade pacing

Enemy level equals the stage number, so enemies gain 20 levels of growth across the campaign. Player upgrades are gated to roughly every 4–5 stages — about 5 upgrades. The two do not meet.

What is needed to close this

The intended design is a sawtooth: difficulty rises between upgrades, and each upgrade restores near-parity. Fitting the enemy curve needs two numbers — how close the gap is at the moment of an upgrade, and how wide it grows before the next one. Those, plus the CP progression spreadsheet, unblock items 5 and 6 above.
