How Combat Power is actually calculated

A traced audit of every input to CP for both player heroes and enemies in Blasty-Stacks — the formula, the authored curve data behind it, how the number grows, and whether it tracks real combat strength. Every figure below was recomputed from the project's own .asset files by reimplementing ProgressionMath, CPWeightMath and CPCalculator outside Unity.

01

The five answers

Short forms of everything that follows. Each links to the section that proves it.

  • How is CP computed?
    A flat weighted sum of six raw stats, multiplied by a melee/ranged flavour multiplier. The weights are not constants — each is an AnimationCurve sampled at the unit's level, so the recipe itself drifts as the unit levels up. §2
  • If CP goes 100 → 120, does every stat rise 20%?
    No — and not even close. Two independent reasons. Each stat compounds a different per-level curve, and CP weights the stats so unevenly that four of the six are effectively invisible. In practice CP ≈ ATK + 0.15·HP. §7
  • Does it differ per hero? Per enemy?
    The growth rates do not. All 8 heroes share one progression config; all 6 enemy archetypes share another. Only the base stat sheet differs — and among heroes, only in two of six stats. What differs for enemies is the level: an enemy's level is the stage number. §5, §6
  • Can total CP be read at runtime?
    Yes — and the code already exists but is unused. Every enemy computes its CP at spawn into a field nothing reads. Players compute no CP at all in battle. CPCalculator.SquadCP exists for exactly this and has zero call sites. §8
  • Is this the right formula?
    It is the weakest of the standard options, and it currently ranks your roster backwards. The genuinely stronger hero profile displays the lower CP. Two well-established alternatives fix it, and one of them is already written in your codebase as dead code. §10
02

The formula

One function, thirty lines, used identically for heroes and enemies.

CP = round( ( wA·ATK + wH·HP + wMv·MoveSpeed + wAS·AtkSpeed + wD·DEF + wR·Range ) × typeMult )

Every w is an AnimationCurve evaluated at the unit's level and clamped to wClamp (−10…10), then scaled by globalWeightScaleByLevel. typeMult is rangedMult for Archer/Mage and meleeMult for Warrior/Horseman. CPCalculator.cs:10–30

Assets/Scripts/Data/CombatPower/CPCalculator.cs — the whole of it

float baseScore =
      w.wA  * s.attack
    + w.wH  * s.maxHP
    + w.wMv * s.moveSpeed
    + w.wAS * s.attackSpeed
    + w.wD  * s.defense
    + w.wR  * s.attackRange;

bool isRanged = s.type == FighterType.Archer || s.type == FighterType.Mage;
float typeMult = isRanged ? w.rangedMult : w.meleeMult;

return Mathf.RoundToInt(baseScore * typeMult);

Note what the formula reads: s is a live UnitStatsRuntime, so the number reflects whatever the stat block currently holds — base sheet, level growth, and any roguelite multipliers already applied. Note also what it doesn't do: there is no interaction between terms. HP and defense do not combine into survivability; attack and attack-speed do not combine into damage output. Every stat is priced in isolation and the prices are added up. §10 is about the consequences of that choice.

03

Where the stats come from

CP is only as meaningful as the stat block it reads. That block is built in four stages — and the menu and the battlefield build it differently.

Growth is compounded, not applied once. ProgressionMath.GetGrowthMultipliers walks every level from 2 to L and multiplies (1 + pct(l)) for each stat, where pct is that stat's own curve. Six stats, six independent curves — which is the first reason the stats cannot possibly rise together.

ProgressionMath.cs:20–45 — compound growth

for (int l = 2; l <= L; l++)
{
    g.gA  *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.atkPctByLevel.Evaluate(l),     cfg.pctClamp));
    g.gH  *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.hpPctByLevel.Evaluate(l),      cfg.pctClamp));
    g.gMv *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.movePctByLevel.Evaluate(l),    cfg.pctClamp));
    g.gAS *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.atkSpdPctByLevel.Evaluate(l),  cfg.pctClamp));
    g.gD  *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.defPctByLevel.Evaluate(l),     cfg.pctClamp));
    g.gR  *= (1f + ClampPct(cfg.rangePctByLevel.Evaluate(l),   cfg.pctClamp));
}

⚠ Two of those six curves are broken in the same way as the flavour curves

In PlayerProgressionConfig.asset, defPctByLevel has its keyframes at t ≈ −9.0 and rangePctByLevel at t ≈ −1.1 — both entirely off the level axis. Because the curves clamp past their last key, Evaluate(l) returns the same value at every level: a flat +6.25% defense and +6.75% range per level, compounding, for all 50 levels. At level 20 that is a ×3.17 defense multiplier that no designer chose. The pctClamp safety net (−0.25…0.50) does not catch it, because the values are inside the allowed window — they are plausible numbers applied at the wrong place.

Does everything grow at the same rate?

The central question, answered twice: once about the stats, once about CP itself.

Reason one — the stats diverge

Attack grows +8%/level early tapering to +3%; HP grows +10% tapering to +4%; move and attack speed grow +2% tapering to +1%. Compounded over 20 levels those small differences become large ones. Note that gMv and gAS are driven by curves with identical keys, so they are always exactly equal — plotted as one line below.

Reason two — CP prices the stats unevenly

Even if the stats did grow together, CP would not move with them, because the weighted sum is dominated by one term. At level 1 a hero's CP of 81 is 79.3% attack and 18.6% HP; the remaining four stats contribute 2.1% between them, and defense contributes exactly zero. Defense never rises above 0.17% of CP at any level.

The direct answer to your question

If a hero's CP goes from 100 to 120 — a +20% move — that is very close to a +20% increase in attack and nothing else. It is not +20% on every stat. Concretely, taking a hero from level 1 to level 10 raises CP by ×1.91, while attack rises ×1.92, HP rises ×2.24, attack speed rises only ×1.18, and defense — depending on which code path you ask — rises either ×1.00 (what the menu shows you) or ×1.73 (what actually fights).

The ranking is inverted

The "fast" profile deals 128 damage per second; the "slow" profile deals 108 — 19% less. Yet the units screen shows the fast profile at CP 81 and the slow one at CP 89. The cause is arithmetic, not opinion: wA·ATK contributes 64 points while wAS·AtkSpd contributes 0.8. Attack speed is priced at roughly 1/80th of what it is worth in combat, so the formula rewards the stat that is displayed rather than the stat that wins fights.

Enemy CP, stage by stage

Enemies use the same formula, but their level is supplied differently — and that is the entire difficulty system.

There is no separate difficulty multiplier anywhere in the project. No stageMultiplier, no hpMult, no per-stage scaling asset. Instead, the spawner hands the stage number to the enemy and the enemy uses it as its progression level:

EnemyManager.cs:90–99 — the stage number is the enemy's level

public void Initialize(int stageLevelFromSpawner)
{
    stageLevel = stageLevelFromSpawner;

    // IMPORTANT: use the stage as this enemy's "unit level" for progression.
    // This is what makes stats grow as you go to higher stages.
    unitLevel = stageLevelFromSpawner;

    RebuildFromBase();
}

So a Golem at stage 20 runs the level-20 growth multipliers: ×3.60 attack and ×4.94 HP on its base sheet. Its defense does not grow at all — RebuildFromBase applies only four of the six multipliers, with a comment acknowledging the gap. Defense is the one axis where the six archetypes have real spread (28 → 78), and it stays flat across all twenty stages while attack and HP quadruple.

A balance observation, offered as data not as a change

The stage-to-stage step is uneven: +91.9% into stage 3 and +29.9% into stage 19, against +3.6% into stage 6 and +3.8% into stage 15. Stages 6 and 15 add almost no threat over the stage before them. Worth knowing: the enemy castle's HP is a flat 350 at every stage from 1 to 20, so the win condition itself never gets harder even as the defending army grows 22×.

SquadCP, zero call sites

Enemies already carry a CP number. EnemyManager.RebuildFromBase assigns cp on every spawn. It is declared as a plain [Header("Debug")] public int cp; and is never read by any UI, any balancing code, or any wave logic — it exists purely as an Inspector readout. Summing it across live enemies would be a loop over the already-tracked enemy list.

Players compute no CP during a battle at all. The only CP call sites in the entire project are six calls inside UnitsPanelController, which is menu code. The battle HUD (HeroStatsPanel, HeroStatCell) shows only an alive/total count and a gem price — the player is never shown a power number, an attack value, or an HP value during a fight. The inputs are all present though: PlayerStatsApplier.CurrentStats is the live UnitStatsRuntime, and HeroRoster already tracks which units are alive.

⚠ The "TOTAL CP" already on your menu screen is not real

Two labels in MenuScene.unity read 3460 and 32660 under a TOTAL CP: caption. Their TextMeshPro components are referenced by no script — they are static art left in the scene, not a computed value.

One caveat if a runtime total is ever wired up: EnemyManager contains a second, divergent CP formula used as a fallback when cpWeights is null. It omits the range term entirely and classifies Mage as melee, so it does not agree with CPCalculator even given identical weights. And EnemySpawner.cs:410 overwrites each enemy prefab's cpWeights with the spawner's own field — so a spawner with an empty slot silently downgrades every enemy in the stage to that fallback.

Is a weighted sum the right shape?

Research only — nothing here is a change request, and nothing was implemented.

The current design is a linear weighted sum: price each stat, add up the prices. It is the most common shape in shipped games and the one with the best-documented failure mode. Two properties are worth naming, because your project exhibits both.

A weighted sum lets one term swallow the others. When one stat's weight is ~20× another's, the score becomes a proxy for that single stat. Yours is at 79% attack. And a weighted sum has no notion of balance. A unit with enormous attack and no survivability scores the same as a balanced unit with the same total — which is why the number stops predicting who wins a fight.

Shop Titans is the closest published analogue: same weighted-sum shape, and its own community's analysis of the formula concludes the metric "is a lie" for evaluating actual hero performance, because the coefficients make ten points of defense worth more than ten points of attack regardless of what the combat model does with them. That is structurally the same failure as the inversion in §5.

The two standard alternatives

B — a product with dampened terms. Pokémon GO's CP is floor(ATK × √DEF × √STA × CPM² / 10). Multiplying rather than adding means a unit that is good at only one thing cannot post a high score; the square roots deliberately stop defense and HP from dominating while still letting them matter. The shape is well-tested at scale.

C — DPS × Effective HP. The analytical option: offense × survivability, where EffectiveHP = HP × (100 + DEF)/100. It is not a heuristic — it is the closed form of "how long does this unit live times how fast does it kill", derived from the damage formula the game actually runs. Your project already contains it. CPCalculator.EffectiveHP mirrors CombatMath's real damage equation exactly, and it is dead code with zero call sites.

CPCalculator.cs:50–54 — already written, never called

public static float EffectiveHP(UnitStatsRuntime s)
{
    float dmgFrac = 100f / (100f + Mathf.Max(0f, s.defense));
    return s.maxHP / dmgFrac;
}

Where this lands

The weighted sum is not wrong in principle — it is transparent, cheap, and easy for a designer to reason about, which is why it is everywhere. It fails here for two fixable reasons: the coefficients are far from the combat model's actual sensitivities, and the two multiplicative stats (attack × attack-speed, HP × defense) are being added instead of multiplied. Shapes B and C both fix the ordering without any change to the underlying stat data. No change has been made; this section is research, as requested.

پنج پاسخ

خلاصه‌ی هرچه در ادامه می‌آید. هر پاسخ به بخشی که آن را اثبات می‌کند لینک دارد.

  • CP چطور محاسبه می‌شود؟
    یک مجموع وزن‌دار ساده از شش استت خام، ضرب در یک ضریب سبک مبارزه (نزدیک‌زن / دوربرد). وزن‌ها ثابت نیستند — هرکدام یک AnimationCurve هستند که در لِوِل همان واحد نمونه‌برداری می‌شوند، پس خودِ دستورِ محاسبه هم با بالا رفتن لِوِل تغییر می‌کند. §۲
  • اگر CP از ۱۰۰ به ۱۲۰ برسد، آیا همه‌ی استت‌ها ۲۰٪ زیاد می‌شوند؟
    نه — و اصلاً هم نزدیک نیست. دو دلیل مستقل دارد. هر استت یک منحنی متفاوت برای هر لِوِل دارد، و از آن مهم‌تر CP وزن‌ها را چنان نابرابر پخش کرده که چهار استت از شش‌تا عملاً نامرئی‌اند. در عمل CP ≈ ATK + 0.15·HP است. §۴
  • آیا برای قهرمان‌های مختلف فرق دارد؟ برای دشمن‌ها چطور؟
    نرخ رشد فرقی نمی‌کند. هر ۸ قهرمان یک کانفیگ پیشرفت مشترک دارند و هر ۶ کهن‌الگوی دشمن یکی دیگر. فقط برگه‌ی استت پایه فرق دارد — و بین قهرمان‌ها، فقط در دو استت از شش‌تا. چیزی که برای دشمن‌ها فرق می‌کند لِوِل است: لِوِل هر دشمن دقیقاً همان شماره‌ی استیج است. §۵، §۶
  • آیا می‌شود total CP را در ران‌تایم گرفت؟
    بله — و کدش از قبل نوشته شده ولی استفاده نمی‌شود. هر دشمن موقع اسپاون CP خودش را حساب می‌کند و در فیلدی می‌ریزد که هیچ‌کس نمی‌خواندش. برای بازیکن در طول نبرد اصلاً CP محاسبه نمی‌شود. CPCalculator.SquadCP دقیقاً برای همین نوشته شده و صفر جای فراخوانی دارد. §۷
  • آیا این فرمول مناسب‌ترین است؟
    ضعیف‌ترین گزینه از میان شکل‌های استاندارد است، و همین حالا روستر شما را وارونه رتبه‌بندی می‌کند. پروفایلی که واقعاً قوی‌تر است، CP کمتری نشان می‌دهد. دو جایگزینِ جاافتاده این را درست می‌کنند، و یکی از آن دو همین حالا به‌صورت کد مرده داخل پروژه‌ی شما نوشته شده است. §۹

فرمول

یک تابع، سی خط، که برای قهرمان و دشمن کاملاً یکسان استفاده می‌شود.

CP = round( ( wA·ATK + wH·HP + wMv·MoveSpeed + wAS·AtkSpeed + wD·DEF + wR·Range ) × typeMult )

هر w یک AnimationCurve است که در لِوِل واحد ارزیابی و با wClamp (بازه‌ی −۱۰ تا ۱۰) محدود می‌شود، بعد در globalWeightScaleByLevel ضرب می‌شود. typeMult برای Archer و Mage برابر rangedMult و برای Warrior و Horseman برابر meleeMult است. CPCalculator.cs:10–30

دقت کنید فرمول چه چیزی را می‌خواند: s یک UnitStatsRuntime زنده است، پس عدد خروجی هرچه در آن لحظه داخل بلوک استت باشد را بازتاب می‌دهد — برگه‌ی پایه، رشد لِوِل، و هر ضریب روگ‌لایتی که قبلاً اعمال شده. حالا دقت کنید چه کاری را انجام نمی‌دهد: هیچ تعاملی بین جمله‌ها وجود ندارد. آیا مجموع وزن‌دار شکل درستی است؟

فقط تحقیق — هیچ‌چیز اینجا درخواست تغییر نیست و هیچ‌چیز پیاده‌سازی نشده است.

طراحی فعلی یک مجموع وزن‌دار خطی است: به هر استت قیمت بده، قیمت‌ها را جمع کن. این رایج‌ترین شکل در بازی‌های منتشرشده است و در عین حال شکلی است که حالت شکستش از همه بهتر مستند شده. دو ویژگی‌اش ارزش نام‌بردن دارند، چون پروژه‌ی شما هر دو را نشان می‌دهد.

در مجموع وزن‌دار، یک جمله می‌تواند بقیه را ببلعد. وقتی وزن یک استت حدود ۲۰ برابر دیگری باشد، آن امتیاز عملاً نماینده‌ی همان یک استت می‌شود. مالِ شما روی ۷۹٪ حمله ایستاده. و مجموع وزن‌دار هیچ درکی از تعادل ندارد. واحدی با حمله‌ی نجومی و بقای صفر همان امتیازی را می‌گیرد که یک واحد متعادل با همان مجموع می‌گیرد — و دقیقاً به همین دلیل است که این عدد دیگر پیش‌بینی نمی‌کند چه کسی نبرد را می‌برد.

نزدیک‌ترین نمونه‌ی منتشرشده Shop Titans است: همان شکل مجموع وزن‌دار، و تحلیل خودِ جامعه‌اش از فرمول به این نتیجه می‌رسد که این معیار برای سنجش عملکرد واقعی قهرمان «یک دروغ» است، چون ضرایب باعث می‌شوند ده واحد دفاع بیشتر از ده واحد حمله بیارزد، فارغ از اینکه مدل نبرد با آن‌ها چه می‌کند. این ساختاراً همان شکستی است که در §۵ دیدیم.

دو جایگزین استاندارد

B — ضرب با جمله‌های میرا شده. فرمول CP در Pokémon GO این است: floor(ATK × √DEF × √STA × CPM² / 10). ضرب‌کردن به‌جای جمع‌کردن یعنی واحدی که فقط در یک چیز خوب است نمی‌تواند امتیاز بالا بگیرد؛ و جذرها عمداً جلوی مسلط‌شدن دفاع و جان را می‌گیرند بدون اینکه بی‌اثرشان کنند. این شکل در مقیاس بزرگ آزموده شده است.

C — DPS × جانِ مؤثر. گزینه‌ی تحلیلی: حمله × بقا، که در آن EffectiveHP = HP × (100 + DEF)/100. این یک حدس مهندسی نیست — فرم بسته‌ی «چقدر زنده می‌ماند ضربدر چقدر سریع می‌کشد» است، که مستقیماً از همان فرمول آسیبی که بازی واقعاً اجرا می‌کند مشتق شده. و پروژه‌ی شما همین حالا آن را دارد. CPCalculator.EffectiveHP دقیقاً معادله‌ی آسیب واقعی CombatMath را بازتاب می‌دهد، و کد مرده است با صفر فراخوانی.

CPCalculator.cs:50–54 — از قبل نوشته شده، هیچ‌وقت صدا زده نشده

public static float EffectiveHP(UnitStatsRuntime s)
{
    float dmgFrac = 100f / (100f + Mathf.Max(0f, s.defense));
    return s.maxHP / dmgFrac;
}

جمع‌بندی

مجموع وزن‌دار در اصل غلط نیست — شفاف است، ارزان است و برای طراح راحت می‌شود درباره‌اش فکر کرد، و دقیقاً به همین دلیل همه‌جا هست. اینجا به دو دلیلِ قابل‌رفع شکست می‌خورد: ضرایب از حساسیت‌های واقعی مدل نبرد خیلی دورند، و دو جفت استت که ذاتاً ضربی‌اند (حمله × سرعت حمله، جان × دفاع) دارند جمع می‌شوند به‌جای اینکه ضرب شوند. هر دو شکل B و C ترتیب را بدون هیچ تغییری در داده‌ی استت‌ها درست می‌کنند. هیچ تغییری اعمال نشده؛ این بخش طبق درخواست شما فقط تحقیق است.
