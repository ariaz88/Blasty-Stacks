================================================================================
  FIGHT EDITING - everything changed in the combat layer, 2026-09-11 / 09-12
  Project: Blasty-Stacks (Stacky Warriors 2D)
================================================================================

Read this before touching combat. It records WHAT changed, WHY, what was measured,
what was tried and rejected, and what is still unexplained. Several items here were
implemented one way, found wrong in play, and redone - those are marked, because
repeating them is the main risk.


================================================================================
  PART 1 - MELEE ENGAGEMENT: units now fight from the SIDE
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM
  When a hero and an enemy lined up vertically, neither could damage the other,
  their sprites sank into each other, and the enemy visibly "skated" into the hero.

ROOT CAUSE (confirmed in the assets, not guessed)
  Every melee weapon is a WIDE, FLAT trigger on a weapon bone. Player_Valkyrie's
  sword box is 7.68 x 1.29 local units offset +4.11 on X; at the visual's 0.19
  scale that is ~1.46 wide by only ~0.24 TALL, reaching SIDEWAYS. Enemy weapons are
  the same shape. Two units stacked vertically are inside each other's attack RANGE
  and completely outside each other's attack BOX.

  Both sides decided "I have arrived" from a plain RADIAL distance, which cannot
  tell "beside" from "on top of".

THE FIX
  NEW  Assets/Scripts/Combat/Shared/MeleeEngagement.cs
       One shared rule for where a melee unit stands:
         Standoff     = 0.85 x range      (where it comes to rest)
         Band         = 0.30 x range      (how far off the target's height it may be)
         ChooseSide   - stand on the side of the target you are ALREADY on
         StandPoint   - the exact spot, always on the standoff circle
         InAttackPosition - close enough, not sunk in, level enough

  THE SIDE RULE IS SELF-CONSISTENT ON PURPOSE. Both halves of a fight run the same
  code, so they must agree or they chase each other sideways forever. "The side I am
  already on" works because A is on the opposite side of B from where B is of A.
  The one ambiguous case is a dead-level approach, and PlayerPreferredSide (-1) /
  EnemyPreferredSide (+1) break it. THEY MUST STAY OPPOSITE.

  DO NOT LET A SLOT FLIP THE SIDE. It was tried. A simulated 2-heroes-vs-1-enemy
  fight drifted 9 world units off the lane in 12 seconds and never landed a hit,
  because the slot-1 hero wanted the enemy's far side while the enemy wanted that
  hero's far side - two demands with no solution. Slots fan UP AND DOWN the same
  flank instead.

  Changed with it: PlayerManager (ResolveAttackDestination, IsInAttackPosition),
  PlayerPursueTargetState, PlayerCombatState, EnemyLocoMotion, EnemyManager,
  MeleeContactRecovery.

VERIFIED
  Simulation against the real authored numbers (hero range 0.85 / enemy 0.83):
  1v1 stacked, 2v1 split lanes, 2v1 same side, 3v3 and 5v5 all settle with ZERO
  movement in the final 2 seconds, every hero in a position it can hit from, and no
  lateral drift.


================================================================================
  PART 2 - THE ENEMY THAT FROZE WITH ITS WALK CYCLE RUNNING
================================================================================

TWO SEPARATE CAUSES, both found by audit.

(a) CODE - the enemy stopped too early
    A "hold position while a hero is engaging us" branch stopped the enemy as soon
    as a committed hero came within maxAttackRange + 0.35 - up to 1.20 world units,
    while the enemy's own stand point is at 0.71. So it froze BEFORE the hero
    arrived. REMOVED. An enemy now walks until InAttackPosition is true, then stops,
    then swings.

(b) ASSET - the walk animation could not be stopped by any code
    Reaper_Man_01.controller and "Zombie_Villager 1.controller" had a
    FreeformCartesian2D locomotion blend tree whose TWO children - 'Idle' and
    'Walking' - were BOTH authored at position (0,0). With every child on the same
    blend point the weights never change, so SetAnimMoving(false) was physically
    incapable of stopping the walk clip.
    Swept all 22 controllers in the project: these two were the only broken ones,
    the other 20 use (0,0)/(0,1) correctly. Both fixed to match. ASSET EDIT.

    LESSON: when a unit "will not stop animating", check the blend tree child
    POSITIONS before touching any code. A 2D blend tree with all children on one
    point is silently unresponsive to its parameters - no warning, no error, and it
    looks exactly like a code bug.

(c) MeleeContactRecovery.Stop() now clears the walk animation, but ONLY on the frame
    it hands the body back. While IsRepositioning is set, both movers skip their
    FixedUpdate, so it is the only thing writing that parameter - and it only ever
    wrote TRUE. Clearing it unguarded would be worse: it runs at execution order
    500, after the movers, and would stamp "idle" over a unit that just chose to walk.

ALSO: enemy movement moved from Update() to FixedUpdate(). MovePosition driven from
the frame clock made step size frame-rate dependent - ~83% of stat speed at 60fps
and ~167% in double-length steps at 30fps. Those oversized steps were the "skating".

NEW  Assets/Scripts/Combat/Shared/UnitDepthSorter.cs
     Y-driven draw depth (z = y * 0.25) so the lower unit is painted in front.
     Every character part sits on sorting layer Default at order 1, so without it
     the tie between two overlapping units resolved arbitrarily and an enemy's head
     could be drawn over the hero. Added in code by both managers, no prefab edit.
     Not a SortingGroup on purpose - that would swallow the world-space HP-bar
     Canvas and drag it into the body's sorting layer.


================================================================================
  PART 3 - THE BATTLE OUTCOME LAW  (a finding, not a change)
================================================================================

The balance workbook (Assets/Documentation for scripts/
Wittle_Defender_Levels_6-10_Balance.xlsx - four sheets, levels 1-10) looks like ten
separate level designs. IT IS ONE FORMULA.

        R = Player CP / Enemy CP

        R <  0.75          ->  Fast Loss
        0.75 <= R < 1.00   ->  Slow Loss
        1.00 <= R < 1.15   ->  Normal Win
        R >= 1.15          ->  Fast Win

  51 scenarios tested across all 10 levels. ZERO mismatches.
  The tiers separate with clean gaps - Fast Loss tops out at 0.735 and Slow Loss
  starts at 0.769; Normal Win tops out at 1.125 and Fast Win starts at 1.154 - so
  the thresholds are read off the data, not fitted to it.

  The win line is exactly R = 1.00. "The player must win" was never a decree
  needing enforcement; it is the statement that this army out-powers that one.

  Charted version: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/396a631b-2c43-4be0-830e-92022f033fe5

WHAT THIS MAKES DERIVABLE (currently still hand-authored)
  - the first winning match per level
  - the win/loss verdict
  - the Slow Loss case (one hard-coded exception naming a level AND a hero count -
    it is just the band 0.75 <= R < 1.00)
  - levels 11+ need only an enemy CP budget and a deployment sequence


================================================================================
  PART 4 - THE COMBAT MODEL: one protected hero
================================================================================

  Exactly ONE hero is assisted: the STRONGEST on the field, chosen when the battle
  is prepared. Every other hero fights with NO assistance whatsoever - it wins,
  loses or dies exactly as it would with the system switched off, and the damage it
  deals on the way is real. That is what makes the fight read as genuine.

  TEAM-TOTAL NORMALISATION replaced per-hero normalisation.
    Scaling every hero to an identical CP made the roster mathematically tied, so
    "the strongest hero" did not exist and the model had nothing to protect.
    Normalising the TOTAL to heroCount x PerHeroCP preserves each hero's share, so
    Player CP still matches the workbook AND a genuine strongest hero exists.
    Individual heroes now VARY around the reference rather than sitting on it.

  The champion is NOT invulnerable and has NO blanket HP floor. Each INDIVIDUAL
  enemy gets a lifetime allowance of how much of it that enemy may ever remove:

        budget per enemy = roundUpTo5( (100 / E) - 5 )%
        E=1 95%   E=2 45%   E=3 30%   E=4 20%   E=5 15%

    Sized so the hero survives even if every ally falls and it faces the whole army
    alone. Spread over at least MinHitsToSpendBudget (8) blows.

  The champion fights whoever is NEAREST, like any other hero - there is no
  assigned duel. The budget is sized against the TOTAL enemy count, so it survives
  whichever enemies it ends up facing, in any order.

  PER-UNIT ENEMY CP for levels 1-3 (authored, not a team budget):
        level 1: 35
        level 2: 35, 35
        level 3: 35, 40, 40
    Hero reference CP: 100 at level 1, 125 from level 2.
    Checked against the law: L1 all Fast Win, L2 all Fast Win, L3 match 1 Normal
    Win then Fast Win - matching the workbook exactly.

  REMOVED and MUST NOT COME BACK:
    - a last-survivor 1%-HP floor that triggered at the END of a fight instead of
      being budgeted before it
    - a damage reduction hard-coded to one specific level and hero count


================================================================================
  PART 5 - HOW MANY BLOWS ANYTHING TAKES TO DIE
================================================================================

  THE RULE: nobody - hero or enemy, any level - dies in fewer than 8 blows, and no
  duel runs past about 11. Each blow lands between 9.1% and 12.5% of the target's
  maximum HP.

  WHERE IT LIVES: CharacterStats.ClampIncomingBlow, applied by PlayerStats and
  EnemyStats AFTER the CP controller has had its say.

  >> IT WAS IMPLEMENTED IN CPBattleController FIRST AND DID NOT HOLD. <<
  A level 4 enemy still died in two hits. AdjustIncomingDamage returns the damage
  UNTOUCHED on four paths that are invisible at runtime: no battle prepared, the
  unit missing from the registered hero/enemy sets, a stale static Instance left by
  a previous battle, or a scene mismatch. Every one silently skipped the ceiling.
  Down in CharacterStats the unit clamps its own incoming damage, so the rule holds
  with no CP battle in the scene at all - which is also what makes it true for
  stage 6+. DO NOT MOVE IT BACK UP.

  Before blaming the clamp, these were audited and ruled out: every hero prefab and
  every enemy prefab applies exactly ONE damage event per swing (one collider
  carrying stats, no duplicate components), there is no bulk-kill or AoE path, and
  the reported video postdated the fix.

  THREE MECHANISMS
    1. CEILING of maxHealth / HitsToKillMe - the divisor is the DEFENDER's own
       number, from its defence.
    2. FLOOR of maxHealth / 11, so a very soft blow is lifted and no duel drags on.
       Suppressed for the protected hero - see the warning below.
    3. LETHALITY GUARD - before the 8th blow the unit cannot be reduced past a
       sliver. Catches float drift and damage arriving outside the weapon path.

  A SINGLE FIXED CEILING DOES NOT WORK, AND THIS WAS MEASURED. With one maxHP/8
  ceiling for everyone, 240 of 240 roster matchups landed on EXACTLY 8 hits - after
  CP normalisation every attacker clears a flat 12.5% blow, so the ceiling binds
  every time and ALL variety collapses. The ceiling is therefore sized from the
  defender's own defence:

        defence 10 -> 8 blows      defence 25 -> 9
        defence 40/52/65 -> 10     defence 78 -> 11

  An upgrade that raises defence walks a character up the band, capped at 11.

  >> THE FLOOR MUST NEVER UNDO A BUDGET. <<
  The floor exists to lift blows that are weak because of STATS. It was applied to
  the champion too, and raised its carefully budgeted 45%/8 = 5.6% blows back up to
  maxHP/11 = 9.1% - so an enemy spent its entire allowance in five blows instead of
  eight and the hero then froze on its reserve. Reported from play as "10% per hit,
  not 5%". ClampIncomingBlow now takes allowFloor, and PlayerStats passes false when
  CPBattleController.IsProtected(this).

  HERO VARIETY IS LIMITED BY THE ROSTER, NOT THE CODE. Every deployed hero is
  authored at defence 25, so they all land on 9. Give them distinct defence values
  and they spread across the band with no code change.

  NOTE ON COUNTING: the band counts TOTAL blows, not blows per attacker. Two heroes
  on one enemy still kill it in eight, so each swings only four times - which looks
  exactly like a broken rule when you watch a single hero. Measured: 8 total, 4 per
  hero, rule satisfied. CharacterStats.ReportDeath() logs one line per death giving
  total blows, the per-attacker breakdown, and the required minimum, so this can be
  settled from the console rather than from a video.

  >> THIS IS WHAT THE 2026-09-12 "DIED IN FOUR BLOWS" REPORT TURNED OUT TO BE. <<
  A level 4 recording showed one enemy taking 8-9 visible blows and another, the
  same level, dying after 4. The Editor log for those exact two battles:

        battle 1:  Enemy_Zombie_villager  10 blows / 1 attacker   <- the one watched
        battle 2:  every enemy            10 blows / 2 attackers

  Nothing was broken. The clamp cannot let anything die before the 8th blow - the
  lethality guard pins currentHP at 0.1% of max until then, and every death in the
  log reads OK. What differed was how many attackers the blows came from.

  THE SECOND ATTACKER WAS NOT FIGHTING THAT ENEMY. Weapon boxes were ~1.5 world
  units wide against a 0.45-wide body, and PlayerDamageCollider / EnemyDamageCollider
  damage EVERY unit they overlap with no check against the attacker's currentTarget.
  In a packed 4v4 line - which is exactly what the recording shows, 8 units shoulder
  to shoulder - a hero hit its own target AND the enemies standing beside it. So the
  victim really did absorb 10, while the hero being watched only landed 4.

  MITIGATION APPLIED 2026-09-12: every weapon box wider than 1.0 world unit was
  narrowed to 0.8 - see Part 12. That makes the extra victim much rarer. It is NOT a
  guarantee, and nobody should treat it as one: two units that end up closer than
  ~1.2 apart can still share a swing. The only guarantee is filtering a swing to the
  attacker's currentTarget, which is still open (Part 11 item 2).


================================================================================
  PART 6 - LEVELS 1-3 ARE A SCRIPTED TUTORIAL EXCHANGE
================================================================================

  These three levels are guaranteed wins fought by one or very few heroes, and at
  those CP ratios real combat reads badly - a level 1 hero at CP 100 against a CP 35
  enemy deletes it in a single blow. So the exchange is SCRIPTED for appearance,
  while levels 4-5 keep real combat and the champion model.

        enemies die on the 8th blow, FLAT, whatever their defence
        heroes lose a flat 7% of maximum per blow
        both are SET, not capped, so it reads the same however units are authored
        the killing blow is taken from currentHP, so float drift cannot leave a
          sliver of health behind

  The flat 8 matters: letting the defence band apply here made a defence-65 enemy
  take 10 and a defence-78 one take 11, which was reported from play. The switch is
  CPBattleController.IsTutorialExchange.

  REMOVED: a "kill in two blows while alone and outnumbered" rule. It only ever
  applied to levels 2-3 match 1 - exactly what the 8-blow script now covers.

  REMOVED: TutorialEnemySpeedScale, a 40% enemy slow-down in these levels. Replaced
  by one uniform authored speed. DO NOT reintroduce a per-level speed multiplier -
  change the stat block instead, so the Inspector keeps telling the truth.


================================================================================
  PART 7 - DAMAGE BUDGETS IN PRACTICE, AND FLANKING
================================================================================

  PER BLOW against the protected hero = budget / 8:
        level 2 (45% budget):  5.63% per blow
        level 3 (30% budget):  3.75% per blow

  BUDGETS STACK. Each enemy's allowance is ITS OWN, and two striking at once may
  spend both. In level 3 that is 30% + 30% = 60%, so the hero drops to 40% while
  the third walks on the base.

  >> A STAGED RESERVE WAS TRIED AND REJECTED. << It unlocked one enemy's budget per
  enemy still standing (level 2: 55% then 10%; level 3: 70%, 40%, 10%) so the hero
  would still be above half when the first enemy died. It froze the hero at that
  floor while the others were alive. The reserve is now the END-OF-BATTLE floor
  only - 1 - budget x enemyCount = 10% - and exists purely so a fully-spent army
  cannot kill the protected hero.
  CONSEQUENCE ACCEPTED: the earlier level 2 guarantee ("hero above half when the
  first enemy dies") no longer holds when both attack at once.

  FLANKING - half damage when genuinely surrounded
        FlankedDamageScale = 0.5      FlankEngageRange = 1.2
        level 2:  5.63% -> 2.81%
        level 3:  3.75% -> 1.88%

    Two enemies on the SAME side is fair - the hero can answer both. Caught between
    two it can only ever face one, so the other hits it for free.

  PILED ON - three or more from both sides  (Arash, 2026-09-12, from a level 3 play)
        SurroundedEnemyCount = 3      SurroundedDamagePerHit = 1% of max HP

    The case that prompted it: ONE enemy on one side of the hero and TWO on the
    other, all three fighting it. Halving is the answer to being caught between two;
    three is a different situation and gets its own number.

        level 3:  3.75% normal  ->  1.88% flanked  ->  1.00% surrounded

    IT IS A CEILING, NOT A SET VALUE, and that distinction matters. At level 5 the
    halved blow is already 0.94%, so "set it to 1%" would make being surrounded by
    five enemies HURT MORE than being caught between two. The code takes the smaller
    of the two, which keeps the progression in one direction - the more enemies pile
    on, the less each blow takes.

        level 4 (4 enemies, 20% budget):  2.50% -> 1.25% -> 1.00%
        level 5 (5 enemies, 15% budget):  1.88% -> 0.94% -> 0.94%  (unchanged)

    The counting is the same five-part test as the flank - alive, in range, clearly
    to one side, its OWN currentTarget is this hero, and in attack position - except
    that it now COUNTS per side instead of stopping at one each. The old test
    returned the moment it had a left and a right, which cannot tell 1-and-1 from
    1-and-2.

    IT APPLIES TO THE PROTECTED HERO ONLY, like every other line in this part. Every
    other hero fights unassisted by design (Part 4), so an ordinary hero surrounded
    by three enemies still takes full damage. That is the model, not an oversight -
    if it should change, it changes in Part 4 first.

    BEING ON BOTH SIDES IS NOT THE TEST. An enemy counts only if ALL of these hold:
      1. alive
      2. within FlankEngageRange (1.2) of the hero
      3. clearly to one side, |dx| >= MeleeEngagement.SideDeadZoneX (0.25), so
         directly above or below counts as NEITHER
      4. its OWN currentTarget is this hero - fighting IT, not another hero it
         happens to stand beside
      5. IsInAttackPosition() - close and level enough to really land a blow

    The LIFETIME allowance is deliberately NOT halved: an enemy still gets its full
    45%/30% and simply needs 16 blows instead of 8 - or 31 at the surrounded 1%.
    Being surrounded buys the hero TIME; it does not make the enemies weaker overall.

    Exposed as CPBattleController.ChampionIsFlanked and .ChampionIsSurrounded if you
    want either on screen.
    Applies wherever the champion is flanked, INCLUDING levels 4-5. If that is
    unwanted there, gate it on LevelBattleRules.IsTutorialPresentation.


================================================================================
  PART 8 - BASES
================================================================================

  A defended base used to take LITERALLY NOTHING while its own units lived, so an
  enemy that walked past the duel and hammered the player's castle produced no
  health drop and no bar movement - which reads as a broken game, not a rule.

        PlayerGateStats takes BaseChipPerBlow = 1% of MAX per connected blow.

  Visible feedback that decides nothing: felling a base that way needs a hundred
  connected blows, far longer than these battles run.

  WHAT DECIDES IT IS THE INTENDED OUTCOME, NOT WHO IS STILL ALIVE
  (Arash, 2026-09-12 - extended from levels 1-3 to every level the system governs)

        battle the player is meant to WIN      ->  1% a blow
        battle the player is meant to LOSE     ->  NORMAL weapon damage
        no prepared battle (stage 6+, or a     ->  NORMAL weapon damage
          gameplay scene entered directly)

    Levels 1-3 are always a scripted win, so they always chip - which is what was
    already asked for and already true. What changed is levels 4-5, where the early
    matches are LOSSES by design: there the base now takes real damage, because that
    loss is the point of the match and damping it would leave the battle unable to
    end the way the workbook says it must. From the first winning match onward, the
    same level chips instead.

        level 4: matches 1-2 normal damage, matches 3-4 chip
        level 5: matches 1-3 normal damage, matches 4-6 chip

    IT REPLACED `HasLivingDefenders`, which asked the wrong question. That damped the
    LOSING battles right up until the last hero fell, and then let the base fall at
    full speed in exactly the battles that were never in danger anyway - backwards in
    both directions. HasLivingDefenders still exists and is still correct for the
    ENEMY gate, which is now its only caller.

    IT CANNOT STRAND A BATTLE. The champion cannot die in a prepared win - the
    reserve floor in LimitDamageToChampion sees to that - so a "should win" battle
    always still has a hero standing to finish it. The chip can never be the only
    thing left running.

  THE ENEMY GATE DELIBERATELY KEEPS FULL IMMUNITY while its defenders live.
  Destroying it ends the level in a WIN, so letting a stray hero chip it would be an
  OUTCOME bug rather than cosmetic - it could finish a match the workbook says must
  be a loss.


================================================================================
  PART 8b - THE SPINNING ENEMY  (2026-09-12)
================================================================================

THE REPORT
  In 4v4 and 5v5, an enemy in the MIDDLE of the line - heroes on both sides of it -
  spins rapidly in place instead of fighting. It looks like a rendering bug.

THE CAUSE - IT IS NOT A RENDERING BUG
  EnemyManager.DetectPlayerTargets runs EVERY FRAME from Update, and it overwrote
  currentTarget unconditionally with whatever was nearest. An enemy with a hero on
  each side is comparing two almost identical distances, so the winner changed frame
  to frame. Each swap flips which side of the target the enemy walks to
  (MeleeEngagement.ChooseSide) and flips its facing with it. What you see is the
  facing flag being rewritten every frame.

THE FIX - A HARD LOCK ON THE ENEMY SIDE
  Once an enemy has a hero it keeps it. A nearer hero does not take it away.
  Released by exactly three things:
        1. the hero dies or its object is gone
        2. the hero is in PlayerLockState (undeployed - the damage collider refuses
           to hit one, so an enemy locked on it would swing at nothing forever)
        3. the hero leaves detectionRadius entirely - which is also what lets an
           enemy give up and walk on the base

  CONSEQUENCE, ACCEPTED: a locked enemy walks past a hero standing right beside it
  to reach the one it locked onto. The two armies meet as a group here, so it is
  rare, and the alternative is the spinning.

THE HERO SIDE ALREADY HAD A WEAKER VERSION OF THIS FIX, AND IT IS STILL WEAKER.
  PlayerManager.retargetHysteresis (0.2) makes a new enemy have to be about 20%
  closer before a hero switches - a margin, not a lock. Its comment records the same
  symptom being fixed there earlier. So a hero CAN still retarget mid-fight, just not
  on a tie. The two sides are deliberately different; if heroes ever need the hard
  lock too, it is the same change in PlayerManager's Phase B.

FREE PERFORMANCE. The search uses OverlapCircleAll, which allocates an array - it
was doing that every frame for every enemy and was one of the larger recurring GC
sources in combat. The lock skips it entirely for any enemy that already has a
target, which is most of them for most of a battle.


================================================================================
  PART 9 - MOVEMENT SPEED
================================================================================

  THE INSPECTOR WAS LYING, AND THIS CAUSED REAL CONFUSION.
    EnemyLocoMotion.moveSpeed and PlayerManager.moveSpeed are FALLBACK fields, used
    only until unitStats is built. The live speed is CurrentMoveSpeed ->
    unitStats.moveSpeed, i.e. the UnitStatsSO. The prefabs showed 0.2 and 0.5 while
    units actually walked at the SO value. All fallbacks are now synced to each
    unit's OWN stat asset so the Inspector agrees with what runs.

  CURRENT AUTHORED SPEEDS (not uniform - set this way deliberately):
        heroes  0.6
        enemies 0.5
        castle  0     (it does not walk)

  REMOVED: a "linearVelocity *= 0.7f when inside maxAttackRange" damping in
  PlayerManager.HandleMoveToTarget. At 0.6 that is 0.42, and it made heroes visibly
  decelerate on their final approach. The stop is still clean because the mover
  halts within 5 cm of its stand point.

  TEMPORARY - PlayerManager.marchSpeedBoost = 1.3
    Multiplies speed ONLY after every enemy is dead - the march on the enemy base.
    0.60 -> 0.78; a ~10 unit lane drops from 16.7 s to 12.8 s. Serialized,
    Range(1,3); set to 1 to switch it off.
    THIS IS A WORKAROUND, NOT A RULE - see Part 11.


================================================================================
  PART 10 - DIAGNOSTICS ADDED (keep these)
================================================================================

  PlayerManager.actualMoveSpeed / intendedMoveSpeed / forwardProgress
  EnemyManager.actualMoveSpeed / intendedMoveSpeed
    Measured from REAL positional movement, not linearVelocity - enemies move with
    MovePosition, where velocity reads a false ~0 and would lie. MeasureSpeed() runs
    BEFORE every early return in both FixedUpdates, so the readout never stalls,
    even during a MeleeContactRecovery sidestep.

    HOW TO READ THEM
      actual ~= intended, forwardProgress much lower
            -> not a speed loss; ally avoidance is bending the DIRECTION
      actual < intended
            -> a genuine speed loss; something is damping it
      actual == 0 while the walk cycle plays
            -> it is stopped and only the animation is running

  CharacterStats.ReportDeath()
    One unconditional console line per death: total blows absorbed, how many
    DIFFERENT attackers landed them, the required minimum, and OK / RULE BROKEN.
    It exists so "it died in four hits" is settled with data.

  CPBattleController.ChampionIsFlanked - public, for on-screen debugging.


================================================================================
  PART 11 - STILL OPEN / UNEXPLAINED
================================================================================

  1. WHY DOES THE MARCH FEEL SLOW? Measured evidence says there is NO speed loss:
     0.59-0.60 fluctuating during the approach, a steady 0.60 on the march. Every
     prefab has linearDamping 0, nothing scales linearVelocity any more, and
     SteerAroundBlockers returns a NORMALISED vector so it cannot reduce magnitude.
     Current best guess is distance plus the total absence of course changes
     (~17 s of uniform straight-line motion vs ~3 s with visible micro-corrections),
     but that is a hypothesis, not a measurement.
     WHEN THE REAL CAUSE IS FOUND, REMOVE marchSpeedBoost RATHER THAN TUNING IT.

  2. SPLASH: ONE SWING STILL DAMAGES EVERY UNIT IT OVERLAPS. Neither damage collider
     checks the attacker's currentTarget, so a swing aimed at one enemy also lands on
     whatever else is inside the box. Two consequences: a cluster advances in lockstep
     and dies on the same swing, and - the one reported from play - a unit's eight
     blows can arrive from a neighbour nobody saw fighting it, so on screen it looks
     like it died in four. See Part 5.
     PARTLY MITIGATED 2026-09-12 by narrowing every box over 1.0 world unit to 0.8
     (Part 12). Units closer than ~1.2 apart can still share a swing.
     THE FULL FIX is restricting a swing to the attacker's currentTarget, which
     removes incidental splash from the whole game - a real combat-feel change, so it
     still needs a decision. Arash chose the hitbox narrowing over it on 2026-09-12;
     revisit only if the ReportDeath breakdown still shows two attackers per kill.

  3. AN UNCAUGHT EXCEPTION PATH. PlayerWaveManager calls CPBattleController
     .CalibrateHero outside any try. ScaleToCP throws ArgumentException when CP <= 0
     and ResetHealth throws when PlayerStats is missing. Playing a gameplay scene
     directly, rather than booting through StarterScene, is the way to hit it.

  4. EnemyDamageCollider has NO duplicate-delivery guard, unlike
     PlayerDamageCollider. No enemy prefab currently carries two copies, so it never
     doubles in practice - but adding one later would silently halve every enemy's
     effective hit count.

  5. Levels 4-5 have not been replayed since the 8-blow band, the flank rule and the
     un-staged reserve landed. Verified by compile and simulation only.


================================================================================
  PART 12 - WEAPON HITBOX WIDTHS  (2026-09-12)
================================================================================

  THE RULE ARASH SET: any weapon box longer than 1.0 world unit becomes 0.8.

  Measured in the Editor rather than read off the Inspector, because these boxes
  live on weapon bones at 0.06-0.51 scale - the authored m_Size.x of 7.68 on
  Player_Valkyrie is 1.46 in the world. The number that matters is
  size.x * transform.lossyScale.x.

  17 prefabs changed; the local size each needed differs because the bone scales
  differ (7.68 -> 4.21 at 0.19 scale, 5.65 -> 2.96 at 0.27, 16.31 -> 8.89 at 0.09).

        Deployed heroes   CowMinotaur_02, Minotaur_02, Pref, Valkyrie
        Undeployed heroes Dark_Oracle_3, Fallen_Angels_02, Golem_3, Minotaur_01
        Enemies           Golem_01, Golem_02, Orc, Reaper_Man_01,
                          Skeleton_Crusader_1, Zombie_villager
        Old characters    Player_ReaperVS2, Player_Satyr, Player_Satyr_Jumper

  LEFT ALONE, deliberately:
    Player_Dark_Oracle_01 (0.984) and Fallen_AngelsVS3 (0.886) were already under
      1.0, so the rule does not reach them.
    Assets/Scripts/TowertDefenseScripts/Prefabs/Player_Satyr.prefab - 0.8 would put
      its own target OUTSIDE its box. Its attack range is 1.25, so it stands off at
      1.063 while a 0.8-wide box centred at 0.621 only reaches 1.021. Legacy prefab;
      narrowing it would make it unable to hit anything.
    The vertical-capsule legacy prefabs (Enemy, Enemy2, Enemy_Golem) - their long
      axis is Y, and they are ~0.2 wide already.

  EVERY CHANGE WAS CHECKED AGAINST REACH before it was applied: the unit's own
  standoff (0.85 x attack range) must still fall inside the new span. Margins after
  the change are 0.18-0.39 world units; the tightest is Enemy_Skeleton_Crusader_1.
  IF A NEW CHARACTER IS AUTHORED WITH A LONGER RANGE, RE-RUN THAT CHECK - a box
  narrower than the unit's own standoff makes it swing at nothing, silently.

  >> SUPERSEDED THE SAME DAY: ARASH RE-TUNED EVERY COLLIDER BY HAND. <<
  The blanket 0.8 was a first pass. On 2026-09-12 Arash tuned all 15 live characters
  himself in the Inspector - size AND offset, per character - and asked that this
  hand-tuned state become the default. It is now the captured one:

        heroes   0.597 - 0.902 world units   (Pref 0.597, Dark_Oracle_01 0.902)
        enemies  0.665 - 0.770               (Golem_01 0.665, Orc 0.770)

  Every one still covers its own stand point. Player_Dark_Oracle_3 is the one live
  character left at exactly 0.800 - untouched by the hand pass.

  THE DEFAULT IS NOW A CAPTURED SNAPSHOT, NOT A NUMBER IN THE SOURCE. The first
  version of the tool hardcoded the pre-narrowing sizes, and the hand pass made that
  table stale within hours. Do not put widths back into source - capture them.

  IT IS REVERSIBLE, BY MENU  (Arash asked for this explicitly)
        Tools/Blasty/Weapon Hitboxes/Capture current widths as default
        Tools/Blasty/Weapon Hitboxes/Restore default widths
        Tools/Blasty/Weapon Hitboxes/Report current widths

    Assets/Scripts/Editor/WeaponHitboxWidth.cs writes size, offset and capsule
    direction to Assets/Scripts/Editor/WeaponHitboxDefaults.json and puts them back
    through PrefabUtility. A capture keeps the file it replaces as
    WeaponHitboxDefaults.prev.json, so the DEFAULT itself has one step of undo.
    Verified 2026-09-12: 24 boxes captured, and an immediate restore reported every
    one unchanged (0.662 -> 0.662, 0.733 -> 0.733, ...).

    Restore deliberately applies WITHOUT a reach check - the values are somebody's
    own tuning, and refusing part of a snapshot would leave the project in a state
    that was never captured. The REPORT item is where reach is judged: it flags any
    unit whose stand point has fallen outside its own box, which is the silent
    failure of narrowing too far. Run it after a tuning pass and after adding a
    character - a new character is not in the file until it is captured.

================================================================================
  THINGS THAT MUST NOT BE REINTRODUCED
================================================================================

  1. A slot that flips which SIDE of a target a unit stands on.
  2. The four-hit / eight-hit rule living in CPBattleController instead of
     CharacterStats.
  3. The damage FLOOR applied to the protected hero - it cancels the budget.
  4. A STAGED reserve on the champion.
  5. A last-survivor HP floor that triggers at the end of a fight.
  6. A damage rule hard-coded to one specific level and hero count.
  7. A per-level movement speed multiplier.
  8. Chip damage on the ENEMY gate while its defenders live.
  9. Two children of a 2D blend tree sharing one position.
